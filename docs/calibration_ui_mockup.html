How to read a calibrated result

Every calibrated number answers three questions at a glance: what range, how confident, anchored to what.

  • Range, not a point. The big number is always a low–high, with the midpoint marked. A single number would be false precision.
  • The band shows the spread visually. The gold tick is the calibrated midpoint; the dashed tick is the model's raw estimate, so you can see how far the data moved it.
  • Wider band = less certain. A wide amber band is more trustworthy, not less — it isn't pretending.
  • Tier chip. High dense data · Moderate partial data · Directional thin/proxy data. Tap it to see what would raise it.
  • Source line. "Calibrated against …" is always visible. Transparency is the point — open the drawer to see prior → data → posterior.
  • Report your outcome. What actually happened feeds back and sharpens the range for the next person in your situation — the loop a population-level tool can't run.
